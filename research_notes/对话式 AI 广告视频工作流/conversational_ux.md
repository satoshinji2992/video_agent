# Conversational UX for a chat-based ad-video agent: feeling like "texting a capable person" vs "AI-ish"

Research date: 2026-10-31. Brain under discussion: Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`, released 2026-09-22). Reliability legend: sources marked "(search snippet, not fetched)" were only seen as search-result summaries; everything else was read in full. Vendor and competitor blogs are flagged as such.

## 1. How do Grok Bot and other messaging-style agents (OpenMausBot, OpenClaw, Meta Muse, OpenAI dots, Manus Cue, texting-native products) design conversation?

### Takeaway
The 2026 personal-agent wave (Grok Bot, Muse, dots, Cue, MausBot, OpenClaw) has converged on one form factor: a messaging app in which each agent is a named contact you "text like a colleague"; it acknowledges briefly, works out of sight on its own computer, and "only comes back when something needs your approval" or when finished work is ready. The "human" feel comes mostly from that interaction contract plus harness features (block chunking with human-like pauses, typing indicators, edit-in-place progress cards, silent `NO_REPLY` turns, quiet hours), and only partly from the model's prose. OpenClaw publishes the most concrete, copyable defaults.

#### OpenClaw (open-source personal agent; origin of the "heartbeat" idea)
- Multi-bubble replies: "Block streaming (channels): emit completed blocks as the assistant writes. These are normal channel messages, not token deltas." Default `blockStreamingDefault: "off"`. Break preference chain: "paragraph -> newline -> sentence -> whitespace -> hard break"; "never split inside fences." — [OpenClaw docs, Streaming and chunking](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/streaming)
- Human pacing: "add a randomized pause between block replies, after the first block, so multi-bubble responses feel more natural." `agents.defaults.humanDelay.mode`: `off` (default), `natural` = "800-2500ms random pause", `custom` = `minMs`/`maxMs`. "Applies only to block replies, not final replies or tool summaries." — [OpenClaw streaming](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/streaming)
- Anti-spam coalescing: OpenClaw can "merge consecutive block chunks before sending them, reducing single-line spam while still providing progressive output"; `minChars` "prevents tiny fragments from sending"; "Discord, Signal, and Slack default coalesce to `{ minChars: 1500, idleMs: 1000 }`." — [OpenClaw streaming](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/streaming)
- Typing: `typingMode` = `never` / `instant` ("start typing as soon as the model loop begins") / `thinking` ("start typing on the first reasoning delta…") / `message` ("start typing on the first user-visible reply activity"). When unset: "Direct chats and group chats with a mention show typing right away." `typingIntervalSeconds` default 6 s "sets how often typing refreshes, not when it starts." — [OpenClaw docs, Typing indicators](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/typing-indicators)
- Voice: the SOUL.md template says "Be genuinely helpful, not performatively helpful. Skip the 'Great question!' and 'I'd be happy to help!' — just help." "Have opinions." "Be resourceful before asking… Come back with answers, not questions." "Never send half-baked replies to messaging surfaces." Vibe: "Concise when needed, thorough when it matters. Not a corporate drone. Not a sycophant. Just... good." — [OpenClaw SOUL.md template](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/blob/master/docs/reference/templates/SOUL.md)
- Personality guide: "if your agent sounds bland, hedgy, or corporate, this is usually the file to fix." Its sharper "Molty prompt" includes "Never open with Great question, I'd be happy to help, or Absolutely. Just answer." and "Brevity is mandatory. If the answer fits in one sentence, one sentence is what I get." It lists as bad rules "'maintain professionalism at all times,' 'provide comprehensive and thoughtful assistance,' 'ensure a positive and supportive experience.' That's how you get mush." It also warns: "Personality is not permission to be sloppy… Sharp is good. Annoying is not." — [OpenClaw SOUL.md personality guide](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/soul)
- Texting etiquette (AGENTS.md template): stay silent when "you would only say 'yeah' or 'nice'"; "Send one thoughtful reply instead of several fragments. Do not respond multiple times to the same message with different reactions." Reactions: "use them to acknowledge without interrupting, express humor or interest, or answer yes/no. Use at most one reaction per message." Platform formatting: "On Discord and WhatsApp, use bullet lists instead of markdown tables… On WhatsApp, use bold or CAPS instead of headers." — [OpenClaw AGENTS.md template](https://docs.openclaw.ai/reference/templates/AGENTS)
- Proactive-message rules (same template): "Reach out when: an important email arrives; a calendar event is less than 2h away; you find something interesting; you have not said anything for more than 8h. Stay quiet (`NO_REPLY`) when: it is 23:00-08:00 unless urgent; the human is clearly busy; nothing is new; the last check was less than 30 minutes ago. When reach-out and quiet conditions both apply, stay quiet." — [OpenClaw AGENTS.md template](https://docs.openclaw.ai/reference/templates/AGENTS)
- Heartbeat is now an ordinary automation: the default "Proactive check" job "runs every 30 minutes…, waits for idle execution, and delivers to a resolved owner DM"; "Return `NO_REPLY` when a check has no visible update"; policies include `activeHours` (timezone-aware window) and `idleOnly` (foreground work has priority). Migration note: "Heartbeat-specific duplicate suppression and no-route skipping were removed: repeated updates can be delivered." — [OpenClaw docs, Heartbeat migration](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat)

- A directory listing describes the intended feel as "short messages, indicators for typing and reading, one-tap functionalities, and contextual memory." — [Slashdot comparison](https://slashdot.org/software/comparison/Poke-vs-Toyo/) (search snippet, not fetched; low-authority source)
- A reviewer says it messages without a prompt and that "Poke also gave itself a contact card within my Contacts." — [Product Hunt review](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/a-week-with-poke-review-a-promising-start-for-a-proactive-ai-assistant) (search snippet, not fetched)

### Inferences
- The shared interaction contract is: (1) a short, human acknowledgement; (2) silent autonomous work; (3) come back only with finished work, a draft to approve, or a real blocker. A chat agent that answers every request with an options table breaks step 2: it hands the work back instead of doing it.
- Message-level defaults worth copying for the video agent, all taken from OpenClaw: split a reply at paragraph boundaries into at most 2–3 bubbles; pause 0.8–2.5 s between bubbles; coalesce fragments so no bubble is one line of filler; start a typing indicator the moment a DM arrives and refresh it every ~6 s; never send "yeah/nice"-only messages; use one emoji reaction instead of a message for acknowledgements and yes/no answers where the channel supports it; on WhatsApp/Feishu-style surfaces, never send markdown tables.
- There is a tension between "several short messages" (OpenClaw's `humanDelay`, Poke) and "one thoughtful reply instead of several fragments" (OpenClaw's AGENTS.md). It resolves as follows: one coherent reply may be delivered as a few bubbles, but the agent should not fire several separate reactive messages.
- Grok Bot's "drafts you approve before they are sent" and "sends screenshots or a clip" map directly onto ad video: the natural unit of conversation is a preview artifact (storyboard frames, a low-res cut) plus one line of context, not a description of options.
- The outer/inner loop split in Grok Bot 101 suggests an architecture: a conversational "producer" agent that owns the voice and the user relationship, with script, storyboard and render workers behind it whose verbose output never reaches the chat.

### Gaps
- xAI does not document Grok Bot's message-level style (typical bubble length, whether it splits replies into several messages, emoji use, typing/read indicators). I found no teardown that measures this; "feels like texting a colleague" comes from marketing and reviews only.
- For Meta Muse, OpenAI dots and Manus Cue I found no primary-source material on conversational style. The only source is a competitor's comparison blog focused on features and pricing.
- I did not research character-style products (Character.AI, Replika, Inflection Pi) in this pass, and found no primary design write-up from Poke on when it splits messages.

## 2. What makes LLM chat output feel robotic, and how to control it through system prompts (Anthropic guidance, Opus 5.x notes, effort and thinking)

### Takeaway
The "AI-ish" register (long answers, headers, bold labels, bullet fragments, option menus, flattering openers, ritual closing offers, hedges) is partly a product of preference training. Human raters reward length and formatting, and sycophancy wins ratings. Consumer apps counter it with system prompts. Anthropic's claude.ai prompt for Opus 5.5 explicitly asks for prose, short casual replies and at most one question per response, but Anthropic states these system prompts do not apply to the API. An agent built on the API or the Agent SDK therefore starts without them. With the minimal default or the coding-oriented `claude_code` preset, it will read like a coding or report agent unless the operator writes an equivalent chat-register prompt. On Opus 5.5, lower `effort` is the main latency lever (thinking is always on), and length must be controlled by prompt, not effort.

### Cited Findings

#### Why models drift toward the AI-ish style
- LMArena's style-control analysis found "Style indeed has a strong effect on models' performance in the leaderboard," "length was the dominant style factor," and "All other markdown effects are second order." When style was controlled, GPT-4o-mini and Grok-2-mini fell below most frontier models while Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude Opus and Llama-3.1-405B rose. — [LMArena blog, "Does style matter?" (2024-08-29, updated 2025-06-13)](https://arena.ai/blog/style-control/)
- The style-control model controls for "answer token length, markdown headers, markdown bold elements, and markdown lists." — [LMArena blog](https://arena.ai/blog/style-control/) (as summarized in search results)
- NN/g defines sycophancy as when "an AI model adapts responses to align with the user's view, even if the view is not objectively true"; it traces the behavior to training on human ratings ("Sometimes, users even prefer false sycophantic responses to truthful responses") and notes that models reverse correct answers when asked "Are you sure?" — [NN/g, Caleb Sponheim (2024-01-12)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sycophancy-generative-ai-chatbots/)
- OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o personality update in April 2025 after it became sycophantic, saying it "focused too much on short-term feedback, and did not fully account for how users' interactions with ChatGPT evolve over time." — [OpenAI, "Sycophancy in GPT-4o"](https://www.openai.com/blog/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/) (via search summary); after the rollback, OpenAI's head of model behavior suggested personality presets rather than a single tone — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/openai-rolls-back-chatgpts-sycophancy-and-explains-what-went-wrong) (search snippet, not fetched)
- Claude's own tic: in July 2025 a developer filed a GitHub issue saying Claude Code says "You're absolutely right!" on a sizable fraction of responses; The Register counted 48 open issues citing the phrase. — [The Register (2025-08-13)](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/13/claude_codes_copious_coddling_confounds/) (search snippet, not fetched)
- An Anthropic study reportedly found Opus 4.7 had roughly half the sycophancy rate of Opus 4.6 on relationship guidance (4.8% vs 10.7%). — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/claude-is-most-sycophantic-while-giving-relationship-advice-finds-anthropic-study/) (secondary, search snippet, not fetched)

#### Recognizable tells
- Wikipedia's AI-cleanup guide catalogs negative parallelisms ("not only… but also"), em-dash contrast phrasing, leftover chatbot phrasing ("Here are several neutral, Wikipedia-style paraphrases:"), hedging disclaimers (which it calls among the most common signs as of October 2026), promotional tone, excessive formatting (bold labels such as "**Recommended:**" and title-style headers), formulaic conclusions ("Despite these challenges", "Future Outlook"), and the rule of three. — [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) (only the first ~40% of the page was read)
- OpenClaw names the conversational tells directly: openers like "Great question", "I'd be happy to help", "Absolutely"; hedging "with 'it depends'"; corporate rules that produce "mush." — [OpenClaw SOUL guide](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/soul)

#### How Anthropic tells claude.ai's Opus 5.5 to talk (consumer system prompt, entry dated 2026-10-27)
- "Claude doesn't always ask questions, but, when it does, it avoids more than one per response and tries to address even an ambiguous query before asking for clarification." — [Claude Opus 5.5 system prompt](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/claude-opus-5-5)
- "Claude avoids over-formatting with bold emphasis, headers, lists, and bullet points, using the minimum formatting needed for clarity. Claude uses lists, bullets, and formatting only when (a) asked, or (b) the content is multifaceted enough that they're essential for clarity. Bullets are at least 1-2 sentences unless the person requests otherwise." — [same](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/claude-opus-5-5)
- "In typical conversation and for simple questions Claude keeps a natural tone and responds in prose rather than lists or bullets unless asked; casual responses can be short (a few sentences is fine). Claude matches its effort to the ask. A simple question gets a direct answer, and a request to change one thing in a longer piece gets the change, not the whole piece again, unless the person asks for the full version." — [same](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/claude-opus-5-5)
- "Inside prose, lists read naturally as 'some things include: x, y, and z' without bullets, numbered lists, or newlines." "Claude uses a warm tone… Claude is still willing to push back and be honest, but does so constructively." — [same](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/claude-opus-5-5)
- Critical scope note: "These system prompt updates do not apply to the Claude API." — [System prompts overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/overview)

#### Anthropic API prompting guidance on format and verbosity (applies to Opus 5.5)
- Latest models are "More direct and grounded: Provides fact-based progress reports rather than self-celebratory updates"; "More conversational: Slightly more fluent and colloquial, less machine-like"; "Less verbose: Might skip detailed summaries for efficiency unless prompted otherwise." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Four steering techniques: (1) "Tell Claude what to do instead of what not to do" ("Instead of: 'Do not use markdown in your response'. Try: 'Your response should be composed of smoothly flowing prose paragraphs.'"); (2) XML format indicators; (3) "Match your prompt style to the output you want… removing markdown from your prompt can reduce the volume of markdown in the output"; (4) a detailed `<avoid_excessive_markdown_and_bullet_points>` block that ends "NEVER output a series of overly short bullet points. Your goal is readable, flowing text that guides the reader naturally through ideas rather than fragmenting information into isolated points." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Explain the reason: "Your response will be read aloud by a text-to-speech engine, so never use ellipses since the text-to-speech engine will not know how to pronounce them" works better than "NEVER use ellipses"; "Claude can generalize from the explanation." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Role: "Setting a role in the system prompt focuses Claude's behavior and tone for your use case. Even a single sentence makes a difference." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Avoid shouting: newer models "are also more responsive to the system prompt… Where you might have said 'CRITICAL: You MUST use this tool when…,' you can use more normal prompting." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Positive examples beat prohibitions: "Positive examples of the communication style you want tend to be more effective than instructions about what not to do." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)
- Name specific anti-patterns: for Opus 5.5 design work, "a general instruction such as 'avoid a generic AI look' mostly swaps one default for another. It responds well to instructions that name specific patterns to avoid." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)

#### Model-specific notes
- Opus 5 (predecessor): "default user-facing responses run longer than prior Opus models'. The effort parameter controls how much the model thinks rather than how much it says: lowering effort can reduce thinking volume without reliably shortening the visible response." Recommended: "Keep responses focused, brief, and concise. Keep disclaimers and caveats short, and spend most of the response on the main answer. When asked to explain something, give a high-level summary unless an in-depth explanation is specifically requested." Plus a `<tone_preference>Keep outputs reasonably concise.</tone_preference>` reminder near the end of long prompts. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)
- Opus 5 scope control: "Make routine judgment calls yourself, and check in only when different readings of the request would lead to materially different work. If the request seems mistaken or a better approach exists, say so in a sentence and continue with the task as asked." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)
- Opus 5 narrates corrections more than earlier models; suggested limit: "For slips that change nothing for the user, make the fix and move on without noting it." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)
- Opus 5.5: "Existing Claude Opus 5 prompts should perform well without changes"; it "generates output tokens more than 30 percent faster than Claude Opus 5 and tends to complete the same task with fewer tokens"; its agentic reports "say plainly what it did, what it found, and what it needs from you." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Opus 5.5 lists the ways an agent ends turns that users dislike, including "an offer to carry on with something unless the user would prefer otherwise" and "a list of decisions for the user when, by your own account, none of them blocks the rest of the work." Its remedy: "your recommendations on open decisions… put them in the same message as your next tool call and carry on with whatever does not depend on the user's answer." Anthropic says to leave this exact block out of human-in-the-loop apps. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Fable 5.1 trend: "Earlier models overused bullets and bold in chat, and many prompts carry anti-formatting rules written to reduce that. Claude Fable 5.1 tends toward the opposite." Suggested replacement rule: "Use lists and bullet points when asked to, or when the content is multifaceted enough that they help with clarity… In conversational, personal, or emotional exchanges, keep to plain prose." Fable 5.1 also gets a "mannered prose" instruction ("Instead of 'a parameter worth varying,' the mannered writer produces 'a dial worth turning'… When a literal phrase is available, use it."). — [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1)

#### Effort, thinking and latency (chat feel)
- "On… Claude Opus 5.5, thinking is always on and adaptive thinking is the only mode"; on easier queries "the model responds directly." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Opus 5.5 effort: start at `medium`, the default on Claude Opus 5.5 (Opus 5 defaulted to `high`). "To get less thinking, lower the effort level first. Lowering effort reduces thinking, and with it cost and latency, more reliably than prompt instructions do." "Changing the top-level `effort` value between requests invalidates the prompt cache"; use a per-message effort change (beta) instead. — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` returns a 400 error on Opus 5.5. For latency-sensitive integrations: "Start at `low` effort and measure… If time to first token still matters after that, a system prompt line such as 'Answer directly without deliberating.' can reduce thinking further." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Chat-specific: "if your system prompt contains instructions that tell Claude to think carefully before answering, consider removing them… In Anthropic's testing in a chat product, removing such a line made replies start sooner, with no clear decline in the quality of the reply." For follow-ups: "Once you have answered something, treat that answer as done. On later turns, focus your thinking on what the user is asking now…" Caveat: "The instruction might also make the model less likely to point out a mistake in an earlier answer." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- General: "Thinking adds latency and should only be used when it will meaningfully improve answer quality… When in doubt, respond directly." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)

#### Agent SDK system-prompt choice
- "Minimal default: when you don't set `systemPrompt`… the SDK uses a minimal prompt that covers tool calling but omits the rest." The `claude_code` preset is "the system prompt that the Claude Code CLI uses." Guidance: "Start from the `claude_code` preset for CLI or IDE-like coding tools where a human watches and steers the work. ### Inferences

#### Likely root causes of the project's "robotic" agent (ranked by expected impact)
1. Missing chat-register system prompt. Through the API/Agent SDK, Opus 5.5 does not receive claude.ai's "prose, short, one question" rules. With the minimal default or the `claude_code` preset (CLI/coding, markdown-rendering terminal), it falls back to a structured, report-like register.
2. The prompt's own style. If the system prompt, skills (SKILL.md) or tool descriptions are written in markdown with headers, tables and bullets, Anthropic's "match your prompt style" note predicts markdown-heavy output.
3. Instructions or tools that invite menus, such as "present options for the user to choose" or "confirm with the user before…", combined with Opus 5.x's documented tendency to stop with decision lists.
4. Verbosity inherited from the Opus 5 lineage. Effort does not shorten visible text, so only explicit length instructions and examples work.
5. Surface mismatch. Tables and headers rendered in a narrow chat bubble (or shown raw in Feishu/WeChat-style clients) read like a document.
6. Harness pacing. Everything arrives in one long message after a silent wait, with no typing indicator and no milestone previews.

#### Draft chat-register system prompt for the video agent (English; adapt names)
Written in plain prose on purpose, following Anthropic's "match your prompt style" advice:

```text
You are Mo, the producer inside [Product]. People message you from their phone the way they would text a sharp freelance video producer: they describe a product or an idea, you turn it into a finished ~30-second ad, and then you keep refining it with them.

Your messages show up as chat bubbles in a messaging app, so write the way a capable person texts a client: short plain sentences, warm and direct, in the user's language and register. Most replies are one to three short paragraphs, and a quick acknowledgement can be a single line. Tables, headers, bold labels and numbered option menus render badly in chat and read like a report, so leave them out; when you need to mention several things, fold them into a sentence. Open with the substance, not with praise or "Sure! Here's…", and don't close with ritual offers like "Let me know if you'd like…".

Make routine creative calls yourself. When the brief leaves something open (music, pacing, aspect ratio, voiceover, palette), choose what best fits the product and audience, say in half a sentence what you chose so the user can redirect, and keep going. Ask a question only when different answers would lead to materially different work and you can't infer the answer, and then ask exactly one, with your recommendation built in, for example: "I'd cut this vertical for Reels. Or do you need 16:9 for YouTube?" When the choice is genuinely visual, use the ask_user tool with two to four options and preview frames instead of describing options in text.

When the user asks for a change, make that change and show the new version; don't restate the whole plan or re-explain earlier versions.

For anything that takes more than a few seconds, say in one line what you're starting and roughly how long it takes, then stay quiet until there is something worth showing: storyboard frames, a first cut, or a problem you can't solve alone. When a version is ready, lead with it, add one or two sentences on what you did and anything notable, and mention at most one obvious next tweak.

Good: "On it. Going for a fast, punchy cut with an upbeat track, about 2 minutes."
Good: "First cut 👇 Logo lands a beat late at the end; I can tighten it if you like."
Bad: "Great idea! Here are some options: | Style | Pros | Cons | … Which would you prefer?"
```

#### Effort and latency settings for chat feel (Opus 5.5)
- Run conversational turns (acks, small talk, simple edits such as subtitle text) at `low`, and creative planning (script, storyboard, reference-video analysis) at `medium`. Switch with the per-message effort change (beta) so the prompt cache survives.

## 3. When the agent genuinely needs a choice from the user, which patterns work better than markdown tables?

### Takeaway
The best-supported hierarchy is: (1) decide yourself and say so in half a sentence, which covers routine, reversible calls; (2) ask one question with a built-in recommendation when different answers lead to materially different work; (3) for visual or costly choices, show a structured card with 2–4 options, preview images and an "Other" free-text escape. The Agent SDK's `AskUserQuestion` supports this natively, including HTML previews. (4) Gate expensive steps behind cheap previews (approve storyboard frames before spending on video generation). Markdown tables of options should never be the mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- One question at most, and attempt an answer first: "when it does [ask], it avoids more than one per response and tries to address even an ambiguous query before asking for clarification." — [Claude Opus 5.5 system prompt](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/system-prompts/claude-opus-5-5)
- Default to action: "By default, implement changes rather than only suggesting them. If the user's intent is unclear, infer the most useful likely action and proceed, using tools to discover any missing details instead of guessing." — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Check in only when it matters: "Make routine judgment calls yourself, and check in only when different readings of the request would lead to materially different work." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)
- Anti-pattern named by Anthropic: ending a turn with "a list of decisions for the user when, by your own account, none of them blocks the rest of the work"; remedy: give "your recommendations on open decisions" and "carry on with whatever does not depend on the user's answer." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Practitioner equivalents: "Be resourceful before asking… Come back with answers, not questions." "Have opinions." — [OpenClaw SOUL.md template](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/blob/master/docs/reference/templates/SOUL.md); "Stop hedging everything with 'it depends' - commit to a take." — [OpenClaw SOUL guide](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/soul)
- Structured choice tool: in the Claude Agent SDK, Claude "calls the `AskUserQuestion` tool" when a task has "multiple valid approaches"; each call has "1-4 questions with 2-4 options each"; fields are `question`, `header` ("Short label… max 12 characters"), `options` (each with `label` and `description`), and `multiSelect`. — [Agent SDK: Handle approvals and user input](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/user-input)
- Visual previews: `toolConfig.askUserQuestion.previewFormat` set to `"html"` (or `"markdown"`) adds a `preview` per option; "Claude includes `preview` on options where a visual comparison helps (layout choices, color schemes) and omits it where one wouldn't (yes/no confirmations, text-only choices)." This is TypeScript only; the SDK rejects `<script>` and `<style>` in previews. — [Agent SDK user input](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/user-input)
- Escape hatch: "Claude's predefined options won't always cover what users want… Display an additional 'Other' choice… Use the user's custom text as the answer value." A `response` field carries a freeform reply when the user dismisses the card. — [Agent SDK user input](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/user-input)
- Long waits on a choice: "If a user might take longer to respond than your process can reasonably stay running, register a `PreToolUse` hook that returns the `defer` decision"; the `PermissionRequest` hook can "send external notifications (Slack, email, push) when Claude is waiting for approval." Limitation: "`AskUserQuestion` is not currently available in subagents." — [Agent SDK user input](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/user-input)
- Product precedents: MausBot uses "answer cards for questions" and "Allow / Deny cards" — [MausBot blog](https://www.openmausbot.com/blog/muse-vs-grok-bot-vs-dots-vs-cue); Grok Bot sends "drafts you approve before they are sent" — [docs.x.ai](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot), plus a "secure form for a password or an API key" — [Grok Bot 101](https://x.ai/bot/guides/grok-bot-101); dots use rules to "act, act if pre-approved, ask, or hand off" — [MausBot blog](https://www.openmausbot.com/blog/muse-vs-grok-bot-vs-dots-vs-cue).
- Reactions as answers: "use them to… answer yes/no. Use at most one reaction per message." — [OpenClaw AGENTS.md template](https://docs.openclaw.ai/reference/templates/AGENTS)
- Channel-native checklists: OpenClaw renders "Plan previews" with "native checkboxes when `channels.telegram.richMessages` is `true`; otherwise they use readable HTML checklists." — [OpenClaw streaming](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/streaming)
- Preview-before-render gating in AI video tools: "Only after approval does the invideo agent attach the storyboard to Seedance 2.0 to animate it"; "Gate by cost — approve cheap frames before expensive video." — [invideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-get-creative-director-approval-on-ai-video/), [invideo FAQ 2](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-stop-an-ai-agent-from-generating-video-before/) (vendor content, search snippets, not fetched); Segwise provides a chat panel to "review, refine, and finalise the AI-generated storyboard before rendering it into a video" — [Segwise help](https://help.segwise.ai/en/articles/16559360-generating-videos-from-storyboards) (search snippet, not fetched)

### Inferences
- A decision ladder for the ad-video agent:
  - Reversible, cheap (music bed, font, pacing, color grade, caption style): decide, state it in half a sentence, continue. Example: "Went with a lo-fi beat; say the word if you want something punchier."
  - Material but describable (aspect ratio, 15 s vs 30 s, voiceover vs none): one question with a built-in recommendation, while doing in parallel the work that does not depend on the answer (for example, writing the script). Example: "I'd go 9:16 for Reels. Or is this for YouTube?"
  - Visual or taste-heavy (look and feel, which reference shot to imitate, which of two hooks): `AskUserQuestion` with HTML previews (thumbnails or keyframes), 2–4 options, a 12-character header, plus "Other". Render it as chips/cards in the app (Feishu interactive card buttons, Telegram inline keyboard, web chips).
  - Expensive or irreversible (full render, paid stock, publishing): approval gate on a cheap preview (storyboard frames or a low-res animatic), approved with a tap or a 👍 reaction.
- What to show instead of a table: one recommendation plus one alternative, both visible as thumbnails. If more than four options seem necessary, the agent has not done enough of the deciding.
- Keep questions non-blocking. The agent should keep working on everything that does not depend on the answer, so the chat never stalls on a menu.
- Because `AskUserQuestion` is not available in subagents, the conversational producer (main thread) should own all user-facing choices, and worker subagents should return candidates to it.

### Gaps
- I found no UX study (NN/g or similar) from 2025–2026 that directly compares option tables with chips, previews or default-and-announce in AI chat. The ladder above is synthesized from vendor guidance and product precedents.
- I did not verify channel limits (for example WhatsApp reply-button counts or Feishu card button limits) in this pass. They should be checked against the platform docs for whichever entry point is chosen.

## 4. How do creative/production agents handle long-running jobs conversationally (progress, completion) without spamming?

### Takeaway
The converging pattern is: one-line intent and ETA, then a single edit-in-place progress card (not new messages) that disappears or finalizes when done, then milestone messages only when there is a preview to approve or a blocker, then a completion message that leads with the artifact, and silence (`NO_REPLY`) otherwise. On Opus 5.5 the harness must explicitly surface the model's between-tool progress notes, which come back as `thinking` blocks that are empty by default. A harness nudge can then ask for an update after several silent steps.

### Cited Findings
- Opus 5.5 writes "short user-facing progress updates: what it just found and what it's doing next," but they "come back as progress-update `thinking` blocks rather than `text` blocks, and their text is empty at the default `thinking.display`. As a result, a client that renders only `text` blocks can look silent during a long agentic turn." Fix: "Set `display: "updates"` (beta, `thinking-display-updates-2026-08-18` header)." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Other levers: give the model "a simple tool for sending the user a message" for verbatim mid-turn content; ask in the system prompt for "a one-line statement of intent before the first tool call and a short recap at the end"; and have the harness count silent tool steps and "after several in a row (five, for example)" append "The user hasn't heard from you in a while — say in a few words what you're doing, then continue." as a turn-scoped system message, stopping "after two or three reminders." In Anthropic's testing this "roughly halved the share of tasks with a long silent stretch, with no measurable change in cost." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Cadence template: "Before your first tool call, say in one sentence what you're about to do. While working, give a brief update only when you find something important or change direction. When you finish, lead with the outcome." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)
- Completion message that stands alone: "Close with a short recap that stands on its own — what you found, what you did, and what's next — so a reader who only sees the last message has the full picture." And: "If your product collapses or hides tool output, tell the model." — [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1)
- Don't mistake a report for completion: "Treat a text-only end of turn as a report rather than as proof the task is done… If something the model started is still running, such as a background command or a subagent… wait for it to finish and return its output to the model as the next user message." — [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)
- Edit-in-place progress (OpenClaw): Telegram's `progress` mode "keeps tool progress in an editable status draft… clears the draft at completion, and sends the final answer through normal delivery." Slack: "Routine progress updates coalesce at one-second intervals; attention and completion flush immediately. The card appears only for turns that do real work, so plain questions are answered without one." Discord: "`progress` is quiet by default: headline, authored commentary and reasoning, plan milestones, and approval requests. Intermediate tool failures and nonzero command exits are hidden." The draft is deleted once the final answer arrives "so busy channels keep no orphaned tool log above the reply." — [OpenClaw streaming](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/streaming)
- Delay-gated status lines: `web_fetch` "arms a five-second timer when it starts: if the fetch is still pending, the preview shows `Fetching page content...`; if the fetch finishes or is canceled before then, no progress line is emitted." Status lines "keep multi-step tool turns visually alive instead of silent." Progress cards default to `maxLines: 8` and `maxLineChars: 120`. Telegram: "Short initial previews are still debounced for push-notification UX." — [OpenClaw streaming](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/streaming)
- Quiet by default: scheduled checks "Return `NO_REPLY` when a check has no visible update"; completion of background work "uses ordinary session execution" so follow-ups return to the original conversation. — [OpenClaw heartbeat migration](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat). Quiet-hours and 30-minute minimum gap rules — [OpenClaw AGENTS.md template](https://docs.openclaw.ai/reference/templates/AGENTS)
- Grok Bot: work continues in the cloud ("Closing the app, your laptop, or your phone doesn't stop a background turn") — [docs.x.ai](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot); bots "only come back when something needs your approval" — [xAI launch post](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot); on completion "the bot sends screenshots or a clip" — [Grok Bot 101](https://x.ai/bot/guides/grok-bot-101)
- User fatigue with over-delivery: "I'm a one-session-at-a-time kinda guy. I hate having to review a ton of agent work (or any work)!" — [Ben's Bites](https://www.bensbites.com/p/grok-bot-is-not-what-you-think)
- Classic wait-time UX: show a progress indicator "for any action that takes longer than about 1.0 second"; looped spinners only for 2–10 s; percent-done for "10 or more seconds"; text such as "Updating address 3 of 50" works for record-based work; users who saw a moving progress bar were willing to wait roughly three times longer. — [NN/g, Katie Sherwin (2014-10-26)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/) (older source, still canonical)
- Agent SDK plumbing: the `PermissionRequest` hook can send "external notifications (Slack, email, push) when Claude is waiting for approval"; streaming input lets users "send follow-up messages during long-running operations." — [Agent SDK user input](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/user-input)

### Inferences
- A message budget for one ad-video job (flows 1–2):
  1. On receipt: typing indicator immediately, then one line of intent and ETA ("On it. Writing a 30s script and storyboard, about 2 min.").
  2. During work: a single edit-in-place progress card with a stage checklist (script ✓ → storyboard ✓ → rendering shots 3/6 → music/subtitles → export). Use a percent or "x of y" once a stage exceeds about 10 s. Status lines appear only if a step runs past about 5 s. Retries and transient errors stay hidden.
  3. Milestone message only if it needs a decision or is a cheap preview worth approving (storyboard frames). Otherwise go straight to the cut.
  4. Completion: lead with the video, then one or two sentences, at most one suggested tweak. Send a push notification if the user has left the app.
  5. Silence afterwards. Proactive pings only for real events (a render failed and needs input, an async job finished while away) and never during quiet hours.
- Route Opus 5.5's `display: "updates"` progress notes into the progress card, not new chat bubbles. Reserve new bubbles for artifacts and questions.
- For flows 2 and 3 (iterative revisions, subtitle edits), quick edits should not trigger the full progress UI. The Slack rule that the card "appears only for turns that do real work" maps directly.
- Keep the heartbeat (from the project's spec) silent by default. A periodic wake-up should end in `NO_REPLY` unless a job changed state, so it never becomes a source of spam.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party write-ups from Grok Imagine, Sora, Runway, Kling or Veo-based apps on how they word progress and completion messages in chat. The creative-vendor evidence (invideo, Segwise) is marketing content about approval gates, not about notification cadence.
- I found no quantitative data on how many proactive messages per day users tolerate from personal agents. OpenClaw's quiet-hours and 30-minute rules are defaults, not research findings.

- NN/g documents that chatbots shift answers to match stated user opinions and reverse correct answers under "Are you sure?" pressure. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sycophancy-generative-ai-chatbots/)

### Inferences
- The project is comparing a finished consumer product (Grok Bot: messaging UX, persona per bot, autonomy, approval drafts, cloud computer) with a raw model inside a custom harness. The evidence suggests most of the gap is product and prompt design, not model capability: Opus 5.5 under claude.ai's own prompt is told to write short prose with one question at most, and Anthropic says that prompt is not applied to the API.
- A fair comparison for the project's 2×2 test would hold the conversational layer constant (same persona prompt, same chunking/typing/progress harness, same choice UI) and swap only the model, scoring transcripts with the lint metrics from section 2 plus blind human ratings of "feels like texting a capable person."
- Warmth should not become flattery. The OpenAI rollback and NN/g's sycophancy findings argue for "warm but candid" (Anthropic's wording: "willing to push back and be honest, but does so constructively"), which is also what a good human producer does: "the logo lands late, I'd fix it."

### Gaps

- I did not verify the GPT-5.x-era personality presets in ChatGPT in this pass, so I cannot cite how OpenAI currently exposes tone controls.
- Most Grok Bot impressions come from early-access users and newsletter authors in August–October 2026; long-term retention and satisfaction data are not public.
